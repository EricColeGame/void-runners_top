Void runners are the archetype you pick when you want to decide where a fight happens — not just how hard you hit. They blink through rifts, punish bad positioning, and disappear before anyone can trade back. Void runners are one of the most misunderstood character concepts in modern sci-fi and fantasy role-playing, and the confusion around Ravnivori makes it worse. Build one wrong and you get a fragile character who dies in every team fight. Build one right and you become the reason your squad wins the map.

## What Are Void Runners?

At their core, void runners are mobile skirmishers who treat space itself as a resource. Where a tank absorbs damage and a sniper holds a lane, a void runner slides between the gaps — literally and figuratively. The "void" half of the name refers to the empty space they travel through. The "runner" half refers to what they do with it: move, reposition, and strike from angles the enemy did not plan for.

In role-playing design terms, this archetype descends from the classic rogue-and-assassin family, but it borrows heavily from teleportation-based classes that became popular as [character class design in role-playing games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_class_(video_games)) evolved toward mobility and positional play. The result is a character who is fast, punishing, and extremely unforgiving of sloppy inputs.

Three traits define nearly every void runner:

- **Phase movement** — short-range teleports, dashes, or rift-walks that ignore normal terrain rules.
- **Burst windows** — high damage packed into a few seconds, followed by a cooldown lull.
- **Fragility** — low effective health, meaning a single mistimed dive ends your fight.

If a character has all three, players will call it a void runner regardless of what the game's official text says.

## Ravnivori: Lore, Rumors, and Community Reports

Here is where things get murky. Community reports about Ravnivori are genuinely inconsistent. Some player experience describes Ravnivori as a named void runner — a singular figure whose story explains how the archetype came to exist. Other discussions treat the term as a title, a lineage, or a faction that trains void runners rather than a single character.

Because the source material does not settle the question, the honest answer is this: treat Ravnivori as a **lore anchor**, not a stat sheet. The name is useful because it gathers the archetype's themes in one place — exile, spatial displacement, and the cost of moving through places people were never meant to walk.

What most community accounts agree on:

| Claim | Community Consensus | Confidence |

## Core Abilities and Playstyle

Void runners live and die by their cooldown management. Every ability in their kit is a tool for changing position, and position is the only stat that actually matters. Below is the ability framework you will see across most interpretations of the archetype.

| Ability Type | Function | Playstyle Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Rift Step | Short-range teleport through terrain | Primary engage and escape tool |
| Phase Shroud | Brief untargetability or stealth | Buys time during cooldown lulls |
| Void Lance | Ranged burst damage | Punishes enemies who hold ground |
| Collapse | Pulls or displaces a target | Sets up kills for teammates |
| Residual Wake | Lingering zone damage | Denies chokepoints and objectives |

The rhythm of a fight is always the same. You poke from range, wait for a defensive ability to be spent, then rift in, land your burst, and rift out. Void runners who try to stand and trade damage lose. Void runners who treat every engagement as a two-second transaction win.

**Actionable tip:** Before every dive, ask yourself what your exit is. If Rift Step is on cooldown and Phase Shroud is unavailable, you are not a void runner — you are a target.

## Build Archetypes Compared

Not every void runner is built the same way. Three broad paths dominate community build guides, and each one asks you to sacrifice something.

| Build | Primary Focus | Strengths | Weaknesses | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rift Assassin | Single-target burst | Deletes isolated targets | Almost no team utility | Duelists and pick-focused players |
| Void Controller | Displacement and zones | Wins team fights indirectly | Lower personal damage | Coordinated squads |
| Endless Runner | Mobility and sustain | Near-unkillable, constant pressure | Slow to kill anything | Objective play and split pushing |

The Rift Assassin path is the most popular and the most punishing. It rewards players with strong map awareness and punishes everyone else. The Void Controller path is quietly the strongest in organized play because displacement scales with team coordination. The Endless Runner path is the safest entry point for new void runners — you stay alive long enough to learn the matchups.

A practical rule: pick Endless Runner for your first several sessions, then move toward Rift Assassin once you can predict enemy cooldowns without thinking about it.

## Team Roles, Synergy, and Practical Tips

Void runners are not solo characters, even when they feel like it. Their value multiplies when someone else creates the chaos they exploit.

| Role in Team | What You Do | Ideal Partners |
|---|---|---|
| Flank Threat | Force enemies to split attention | Frontline tanks who draw cooldowns |
| Pick Artist | Isolate overextended targets | Long-range controllers |
| Objective Disruptor | Contest zones with residual damage | Healers who can reach you mid-dive |
| Cleanup | Finish fights after the main exchange | Burst casters who soften targets |

**Habits that separate good void runners from great ones:**

- **Track one cooldown at a time.** You do not need to memorize everything — just the one ability that kills you.
- **Dive from an angle, never from the front.** A void runner walking straight in is just a squishy melee.
- **Bank your escape.** If you use Rift Step to engage, you have already made a mistake.
- **Play the second fight.** Void runners are strongest when the enemy's defensive tools are already spent.
- **Watch the map, not your character.** Your character is where you left it; the map is where the game is.

Common mistakes and their fixes:

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Diving without an exit | Instant death, no trade value | Hold one mobility cooldown in reserve |
| Chasing kills across the map | Abandons objectives | Let runners come to you |
| Building pure damage | No survivability in extended fights | Add one defensive or sustain option |
| Ignoring residual zones | Wastes your best area denial | Drop zones on objectives, not on players |
| Fighting when cooldowns are down | You are the weakest character on the field | Disengage and reset |

If you only remember one thing, remember that void runners are a **patience archetype disguised as an aggressive one**. The players who look reckless are almost always counting cooldowns in their head.

## FAQ

**Is Ravnivori a void runner or a separate character?**
Community reports disagree, and no authoritative source settles it. The most useful reading is that Ravnivori represents the origin or faction behind void runners rather than a single playable figure. Treat the name as lore context, not a stat block.

**Are void runners good for beginners?**
They are not the easiest archetype, but they are learnable. Start with a mobility-and-sustain build, focus on staying alive, and add burst damage only once you can reliably predict when enemies will commit to a fight.

**What is the biggest mistake new void runners make?**
Spending their escape ability to start a fight. A void runner with no exit is just a low-health character standing in the wrong place. Always keep one movement tool available for the retreat.

**Do void runners work in solo play?**
Yes, with adjustments. In solo queues, lean toward builds with self-sustain and objective pressure rather than pure assassination. The Rift Assassin path depends on teammates following up, which you cannot count on in uncoordinated matches.

**How do I know if a character counts as a void runner?**
Check for the three core traits: phase movement, a burst window, and low durability. If a character has all three, the community will treat it as part of the archetype regardless of its official label.
