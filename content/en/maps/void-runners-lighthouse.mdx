## What Void Runners Is, According to the Store Page

The official listing at [Void Runners on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5334520/) describes a co-op salvage raid for one to four pilots, played either on a single couch or online. The headline mechanic is the beam. Each pair of ships is joined by an energy beam, and that beam shreds anything it sweeps. You are not just flying a ship and shooting; you are dragging a cutting line across the map, and the line is attached to another human being.

That single design choice reshapes everything. Where you fly is no longer a personal decision, because your position also determines where the beam is and what it cuts. Spreading out and swinging the web are the store page's own words for how a crew is meant to operate. The page also mentions crossfire, dodging, reviving crew from escape pods, and building a salvaged ship as you go.

Run structure is stated plainly. A run crosses three sectors of four jumps each, ending at a guardian. Three sectors, three guardians. That is the advertised shape of a campaign attempt, and it is a short, dense structure rather than a long crawl.

## Why the Beam Is the Whole Game

Most twin-stick roguelikes ask you to aim and move at the same time. Void Runners asks you to aim, move, and consider where your linked partner is, often simultaneously. The beam is not a bonus you occasionally fire. It is a persistent connection between two ships, which means the geometry of your formation is the real weapon.

This produces an interesting tension that the store description implies but does not spell out. If you spread out to cover more ground, the beam stretches and sweeps a wider arc, but each pilot has less support. If you cluster, the beam is short and tight, and you lose the sweeping coverage that makes the mechanic useful. There is no documented "correct" distance, so the practical answer is going to be situational and crew-dependent.

The reviving system adds a second layer. When a pilot goes down, they can be recovered from an escape pod rather than simply respawning. That means a wiped pair is not necessarily a lost run, but it also means someone has to break formation to retrieve a pod, which changes the beam geometry for everyone still flying. Cooperative recovery is baked into the risk calculation rather than bolted on.

## Practical Reasoning for a Crew of One to Four

Since the mechanics are crew-relative, the useful advice is about formation rather than builds.

With two pilots, the beam is your only beam, so every decision is a trade. Sweeping a wide arc clears space but leaves both ships exposed. A tighter loop is safer but slower. The escape pod revival means you can gamble more than you would in a game with permadeath on each pilot, provided whoever is still up is willing to fly into danger to retrieve the pod.

With three or four pilots, the store page's framing of "swinging the web" becomes literal. Multiple beams can cross, which is where crossfire enters the picture. The risk is that a dense web is also a dense hazard: if a beam is shredding everything it sweeps, an ally's beam is not obviously safe just because it is friendly. That specific interaction is not documented, so treat it as an open question to test in-game rather than an assumed rule.

For a solo pilot, the page lists one to four players, which suggests solo play is supported, but it does not explain how the beam mechanic adapts when there is no partner. That is another undocumented area. Do not assume the beam disappears or attaches to a drone until the game says so.
