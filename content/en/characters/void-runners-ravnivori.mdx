## The Beam-Link Mechanic, Explained From the Store Description

Most twin-stick co-op games treat each player as an independent gun. Void Runners instead joins pairs of ships with a beam, and that beam damages whatever it sweeps across. The store description frames the whole experience around this: spreading out and swinging the web, catching enemies in crossfire, dodging, and reviving crew from escape pods.

The practical consequence is that your position relative to your crewmates is not just a safety question, it is your primary weapon. A lone pilot has no beam partner, so solo play likely reads very differently from a full four-pilot run, though the store page does not detail how a single ship is balanced. Two pilots can sweep a line across a room. Four pilots can plausibly form multiple link pairs and overlap their sweeps, which the description gestures at with the word "web."

What the description does not give us: beam length, damage values, whether beams are always on, whether they can be toggled or redirected, or what happens when a linked partner is destroyed. None of that is public. If you want to know before buying, the honest answer is that you cannot verify it until the game ships or the developer posts more detail.

## Why "Where You Fly" Is the Real Skill Ceiling

If the beam is the weapon, then movement is the aim. That flips a lot of habits. In most twin-stick games you dodge to survive and shoot to kill; here, dodging and killing are the same input. Swinging a beam around a cluster of enemies does damage, but so does dragging it across a downed teammate's escape pod area while you reposition.

Three implications follow directly from the advertised features, without needing any unreleased numbers:

- Spacing is a shared resource. Two pilots hugging each other sweep almost no area. Two pilots at maximum separation sweep a lot, but each has less support.
- Crossfire is a positioning achievement, not a stat. The description's mention of crossfire suggests overlapping sweeps are the payoff for coordinated movement.
- Revives change the math. Crew come back from escape pods, so losing a link partner is a tempo problem, not necessarily a run-ending one.

Note the word "suggests." The store page says crossfire and reviving are in the game; it does not explain the rules. I am reasoning about incentives, not reporting mechanics.
