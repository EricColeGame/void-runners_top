## What Void Runners Actually Is

The official Steam description frames Void Runners as a co-op salvage raid for one to four pilots, played either on one couch or online. It is a twin-stick shooter with roguelike structure: you fly, you shoot, you die, you try again with what you learned. A run crosses three sectors, each four jumps long, ending at a guardian. Three sectors and three guardians deep is how the store page puts it.

The defining mechanic is the beam. Each pair of ships is joined by a beam that shreds whatever it sweeps. That single sentence changes how you think about positioning. In a normal twin-stick game, your ship's location matters because of what you can shoot. Here, your location matters because of what your beam crosses, and that line is shared with another player. Where you fly relative to your crew is the core decision, not a side detail.

If you want to read the source yourself, the official page is at https://store.steampowered.com/app/5334520/. Anything not stated there or in the developer's own materials should be treated as unconfirmed.

## How the Beam Changes Team Play

Advertised features point to a handful of concrete behaviors:

- Spreading out and swinging the web: the store description uses this language, implying that distance between linked ships shapes the beam's reach and path.
- Crossfire: overlapping beams and shots appear to be a designed tactic rather than an accident.
- Dodging: your own beam presumably does not distinguish friend from foe, so movement discipline matters.
- Reviving crew from escape pods: death is not necessarily final for the squad.

What is not documented is the exact geometry, tick rate, damage falloff, or whether beams can be oriented independently of ship facing. Those are mechanical specifics the store page does not publish. Players will only be able to answer them by flying the game themselves or watching developer footage, and until Q1 2027 that footage may not exist. A reasonable approach for now is to treat the beam as a shared resource: two pilots who both drift toward the same enemy are effectively stacking one line, while two who separate create two cutting paths. That is inference from the advertised description, not a confirmed damage model.
