## What Void Runners Is, According to the Store

The official description frames Void Runners as co-op salvage raids for one to four pilots, played either on one couch or online. You fly ships through hostile space, and each pair of ships is joined by a beam. That beam shreds whatever it sweeps. So the beam is not just a tether or a visual flourish; it is the weapon, and its arc is determined by where two players choose to fly relative to each other.

The structure is stated just as plainly: a run crosses three sectors, each four jumps deep, ending at a guardian, and there are three guardians in total. The store also mentions spreading out and swinging the web, crossfire, dodging, reviving crew from escape pods, and building a salvaged ship.

That is the whole advertised feature set. Notice what is *not* there: no ship part list, no weapon stats, no boss names, no sector maps, and no code system. If a search result promises you any of those, it is either guessing or describing a different game.

## Why the Beam Geometry Is the Real Mechanic

Most twin-stick roguelikes ask one question per second: where do I point? Void Runners adds a second question: where do I *stand*? Because the beam links two ships, its damage output depends on a relationship, not a single input. That changes three things immediately.

First, positioning becomes a shared resource. If you crowd your partner, your beam is short and your sweep is narrow. If you spread across the screen, the beam stretches into a long line that covers more ground as you move. The store's phrase "spreading out and swinging the web" reads like a direct hint that distance is the point.

Second, friendly fire is implied by the mechanic itself. A beam that shreds "anything it sweeps" does not obviously exempt your own hull. Whether beams damage their own pilots is not documented, and that is the single most important thing to test on day one.

Third, revives create tempo decisions. Downed pilots return via escape pods, which the store lists as a feature. That means a death is not automatically a run-ender, but someone has to break formation to collect the pod. In a two-player run, that means giving up beam coverage; in a four-player run, it means deciding who peels off.

## Solo and Four-Player Are Different Games

The description covers one to four pilots, which spans three very different playstyles, and the beam logic scales with each.

Solo, the question is whether a lone pilot has any beam at all. The store does not say. A single ship has no pair, so either the game grants a solo alternative or the one-player mode is a different ruleset entirely. Do not assume the co-op geometry carries over.

Two players is the cleanest expression of the idea: one beam, one shared arc, constant negotiation over spacing.

Three players is the awkward case, because "each pair of ships is joined by a beam" can mean a chain of two beams, a triangle of three, or something else. In a triangle, every pilot is anchored to two partners at once, which makes spreading out much harder and crossfire much easier.

Four players is the maximum stated. More ships means more beams, more crossfire, and more chances for a beam to sweep a teammate. Voice chat is not mentioned as a built-in feature, so plan on whatever your platform provides.
