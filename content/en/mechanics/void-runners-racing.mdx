"Void Runners" is an easy name to confuse with something else. Searches for it turn up void-themed games of every genre, most of which have nothing to do with this one. The Void Runners in question is a Steam game by developer and publisher Aaron Reisman (Steam app 5334520), announced for Q1 2027 and currently marked "coming soon." ## The Core Idea: Pairs of Ships, Joined by Beams

The advertised hook is geometry. Void Runners is described as a co-op salvage raid for one to four pilots, played on one couch or online. Each pair of ships is linked by a beam that shreds anything it sweeps. That single sentence reframes the whole genre: in a normal twin-stick shooter, you ask "where do I fly?" Here you also ask "where am I relative to my crew?"

Because the beam exists only between linked ships, the damage you deal is a function of two players' positions at once, not one player's aim. Spreading out stretches the beam into a long line that covers ground but moves slowly across the screen as angles change. Flying close makes the beam short and possibly harder to keep on a target. The store description explicitly names spreading out, swinging the web, and crossfire as part of the intended play, which suggests the design rewards coordinated positioning over individual twitch skill.

What the page does not say is how many beams exist at four players, whether links are fixed or reconfigurable, or whether a solo pilot controls one ship or a linked pair. Those are documented nowhere publicly as of this writing.

## A Run: Three Sectors, Four Jumps, One Guardian

The advertised run structure is short and legible: three sectors, each four jumps long, ending at a guardian. That is twelve jumps and three guardian encounters if you clear the whole thing. The store page frames the goal as going "three sectors and three guardians deep," not as an endless mode.

Two cautions here. First, the page does not name the guardians, describe their attack patterns, or state whether guardians are mandatory gates or optional fights. Second, "jump" is not defined in the store text; it could mean a discrete combat room, a node on a map, or a transition. Anyone claiming to know the exact sector layouts is working from something other than the official description.

## Escape Pods, Salvage, and Building a Ship

Two systems appear in the store description and are worth separating from the combat hook. The first is death handling: crew are revived from escape pods rather than simply respawning. That implies a downed pilot leaves something behind that a teammate must reach, which turns a wipe into a rescue problem — and, given the beam mechanic, likely a repositioning problem too.

The second is progression: players build a salvaged ship. "Salvaged" is the operative word, implying parts are recovered from what you destroy or find rather than bought from a menu. That is consistent with the salvage-raid framing.

Beyond that, nothing is public. The page does not publish ship parts, weapon stats, rarity tiers, currency, unlock costs, or a persistent progression system. If you see a list of named modules with damage numbers attributed to Void Runners, treat it as unreliable unless it comes from Reisman.

## Practical Reasoning for Players Planning Ahead

If you are deciding whether to follow this game, a few grounded takeaways:

1. **Plan for a group of two or four.** The beam-pair mechanic is the identity of the game. One player and four players are both advertised, but the interesting decisions described all involve crew positioning, so a duo is the natural minimum for the intended experience.
2. **Expect short runs.** Twelve jumps across three sectors is a compact structure by roguelike standards. If you prefer long, sprawling runs, this may not be the shape you want.
3. **Local co-op is a real option.** "One couch or online" is explicit, which matters if you want to play without networking.
4. **Do not buy build guides.** There are no parts or stats published, so no build advice can be current.
5. **Wishlist rather than preorder-watch.** The page lists Q1 2027, and release timing for small-team projects moves. The store page is where a change would appear first.

None of this guarantees the game will play well; it only describes what has been promised.
