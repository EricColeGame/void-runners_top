## What Are Void Runners?

Void runners are characters who fight by stepping outside reality — blinking through rifts, collapsing space around a target, and turning an empty arena into a kill zone. Ask most players to name a void runners Kiana example and they will point straight at Kiana Kaslana's void-themed forms, and for good reason: those kits made the archetype famous. This guide breaks down what void runners actually do, why Kiana became their reference point, and how to build and play them without wasting resources.

The "void" in the name does two jobs at once. It is a power source — a character draws strength from emptiness, collapsed space, or anti-matter — and it is a movement promise. Void runners are typically the fastest members of a team and, almost by design, the most fragile. That trade-off is the whole point: you are trading safety for tempo.

| Core Trait | How It Shows Up in Play | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Rift mobility | Short-range teleports, blink dashes, phasing through hazards | Escape tools double as repositioning for damage |
| Space control | Gravity wells, pull effects, lingering damage zones | Groups enemies so one rotation hits everything |
| Burst windows | High output gated behind a short uptime state | Rewards setup and timing over button mashing |
| Fragility | Low defense, self-damage, or health costs | Punishes greedy play, rewards clean dodges |
| Resource tension | Energy that drains while transformed | Forces a real decision about when to commit |

### Why the Archetype Keeps Coming Back

Designers love void runners because they solve an old problem: how do you make a character feel fast without making them feel weightless? The answer is consequence. A blink that costs energy, a transformation that drains while active, a dodge window that is generous but not infinite — all of it keeps speed from becoming a free pass. When those costs are tuned well, the character feels powerful and precise rather than slippery.

## How Void Runners Actually Play

### Mobility Is a Damage Stat

New players treat the blink as a panic button. Experienced players treat it as a rotation tool. The difference is huge. If your dash repositions you into a better angle, it is not a defensive cooldown — it is the first hit of your combo. Practice using rift movement *toward* a target's flank rather than away from it, and only burn the escape charge when the animation has already committed.

### Burst Windows Reward Setup, Not Reflexes

Void runners usually have a short window where their damage spikes. The mistake is popping it the moment it is available. The better habit is to spend a few seconds grouping enemies, applying your debuff or pull, and *then* committing. A burst window that hits five clustered targets is worth far more than one that hits a single straggler.

### Manage the Cost of Power

Most void-themed kits carry an upkeep cost — energy drain, health loss, or a stacking penalty. Track that cost the same way you track your health bar. If you enter a transformation with a nearly empty resource pool, you will exit it mid-fight with nothing to show for it. Bank first, then burst.

## Build Priorities for Void Runner Characters

Void runners are stat-hungry. They want damage, they want uptime, and they want just enough survivability to survive one mistake. The ordering below is a general priority list for the archetype; adjust it once you know your team's buffs.

| Priority | What to Look For | Why It Comes First |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Burst uptime | Cooldown reduction, energy regeneration | More windows means more total damage |
| 2. Damage multipliers | Elemental or type bonuses that match the kit | Amplifies everything else you invest in |
| 3. Mobility support | Effects that trigger on dash or dodge | Turns repositioning into free value |
| 4. Sustain | Shields, healing on hit, damage reduction | Buys the one mistake you will make |
| 5. Utility | Crowd control, debuff application | Helps the team, but rarely fixes a bad rotation |

| Team Role | What the Void Runner Needs | Common Pairing |
|---|---|---|
| Main damage | A dedicated buffer and a crowd-control enabler | Support + gather unit |
| Sub-damage | A short field time with a strong swap-in | Burst carry |
| Enabler | Fast application of a debuff or pull effect | Any high-multiplier damage dealer |

### A Practical Rotation Template

1. Enter with a gap-closer so you start inside the fight, not walking toward it.
2. Apply your pull, gravity well, or grouping effect.
3. Land your debuff or setup skill while enemies are still clustered.
4. Commit to the burst window and stay mobile inside it.
5. Exit before the resource drains to zero, then reset positioning.

That fifth step is where most players lose damage. Leaving a transformation early feels wasteful, but a clean exit keeps you alive for the next window.

## Common Mistakes With Void Runner Kits

## FAQ

**Are void runners good for beginners?**
Generally, no — they are a mid-to-advanced archetype. Their damage depends on timing windows and resource management, so a player who is still learning enemy patterns will get more value from a forgiving bruiser. Once dodge timing clicks, void runners become some of the most rewarding characters to pilot.

**Why is Kiana always mentioned alongside void runners?**
Because her void-themed forms popularized the template. Community discussion around void runners Kiana kits tends to focus on the same themes: high mobility, a temporary power state, and a real cost for using it. She is the clearest teaching example of the archetype.

**Do I need the rarest gear to make a void runner work?**
No. Priority order matters more than rarity. Burst uptime and matching damage multipliers do most of the heavy lifting, and a well-timed rotation with average gear will outperform a sloppy rotation with perfect gear.

**What is the fastest way to improve?**
Record one run, then watch only your exits. Most void runner players find their biggest losses happen in the two seconds after a transformation ends, not during the burst itself. Fix the exit, and your damage numbers follow.
