## What Void Runners Advertises

According to the Steam listing, Void Runners is a twin-stick roguelike for one to four players, playable on a single couch or online. The hook is the beam. Ships are linked in pairs by a beam that shreds anything it sweeps, so your position is never just about you; it is about the line between you and your partner. The description frames the experience around spreading out and swinging the web, crossfire, dodging, and reviving crew from escape pods. Runs are structured as three sectors and three guardians deep, and there is a ship you build from salvage as you go.

That is the whole verified feature set. Notice how much it leaves open. There is a run structure — three sectors, three guardians — but no sector names, no jump-by-jump map, no guardian names. There is a salvage-based ship, but no parts list, no slot system, no upgrade paths. There is a beam, but no damage numbers. ## Why the Beam Is the Whole Game

Most twin-stick roguelikes ask where you should be. Void Runners asks where *we* should be. If the beam is the primary damage tool, then every dodge, every retreat, and every greedy push is a decision about the geometry between two or more ships. Fly too close and you lose the sweep. Fly too far and you may lose the link or leave a partner exposed. Swing the web and the beam covers new ground, but the arc is predictable, which means enemies can punish it.

The practical reading of the store text is that coordination is the difficulty curve. A solo pilot controls one ship, and it is not yet documented how a lone player handles a paired beam. That is a real open question and worth checking on the store page or in any later developer update rather than guessing. For crews, the advertised escape-pod revive loop implies death is not the end of a run; losing a ship costs you position and tempo while someone recovers the pod, which makes the beam weaker exactly when things are worst. That is a design inference from the description, not a stated rule.
