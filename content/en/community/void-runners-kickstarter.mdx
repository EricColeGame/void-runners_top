Void Runners is an unreleased twin-stick roguelike from solo developer and publisher Aaron Reisman, listed on Steam as app 5334520 with a Q1 2027 release window and a "coming soon" status. The hook is not the shooting. It is the tether. Each pair of ships in a crew is joined by a beam that shreds whatever it sweeps, which means the most important decision in any firefight is not which enemy you shoot but where you position your ship relative to your crewmates. That single design choice reshapes everything a co-op roguelike normally asks of its players.

If you have played twin-stick shooters, you already have the muscle memory for aiming and dodging. Void Runners asks you to unlearn part of it. Your spacing is a weapon.

## Why a Shredding Beam Changes Positioning

In a typical four-player twin-stick roguelike, players spread out to cover angles. Spatial separation is almost always good: you cover more of the arena, you avoid splash damage, and you can flank.

A beam that shreds anything it sweeps inverts part of that logic. Separation still gives you coverage, but it also defines a live cutting surface between you and your partner. That line is not decorative. It is damage output that exists only while the geometry is right, and it moves the instant either pilot moves.

The practical consequence is that your positioning decision has two outputs at once. Where you fly determines both your own survival and the shape of a damaging line somewhere else on the screen. Pilots who think only about their own dodge will leave the beam pointing at empty space. Pilots who think only about the beam will fly into fire.

There is no published damage value, tick rate, or beam width in the official listing, so treat any specific numbers you see elsewhere as unverified. What the store page supports is the qualitative claim: sweeping the beam into enemies is the point, and spreading out to swing the web is described as the playstyle.

| Decision | Selfish reading | Crew reading |
|---|---|---|
| Spread wide | More personal space to dodge | Longer beam, wider sweep arc |
| Stay tight | Easier to revive quickly | Shorter beam, less coverage |
| Rotate around a partner | Opens your own firing lane | Sweeps the beam across a crowd |
| Retreat to a corner | You survive longer | Beam may point away from the fight |

This table is reasoning from the advertised mechanic, not a published strategy guide. The trade-offs are inherent to any linked-ship design; the specific numbers behind them are not public.

## The Three-Sector Run and What It Implies

The store page does not say how long a run takes, whether sectors are procedurally generated, whether you can choose jump destinations, or what a guardian fight looks like beyond the label.

What the structure does tell you is that a Void Runners run is short enough that a failed attempt is not a catastrophic time investment. That matters for a game built around a mechanic you have to feel rather than read. Beam spacing is a coordination skill, and coordination skills improve through repetition with the same people. A short run cadence supports that.

It also means the escape pod revival system carries weight. The store description mentions reviving crew from escape pods, so a death is not necessarily the end of a run — but the listing says nothing about how many revives are available, whether they cost anything, or whether they work differently for a solo pilot. All of that is undocumented.
