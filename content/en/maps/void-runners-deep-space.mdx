## What the store page actually advertises

Steam's appdetails data (retrieved October 14, 2026) gives a compact but specific picture. Void Runners is a twin-stick roguelike for one to four pilots, playable on one couch or online. The defining mechanic is that each pair of ships is joined by a beam, and that beam shreds whatever it sweeps. That means your position relative to your crewmates is not cosmetic. It is the decision the game is built around.

The description also mentions spreading out and swinging the web, crossfire, dodging, and reviving crew from escape pods. Structurally, a run crosses three sectors of four jumps each, ending at a guardian, and the store text refers to three guardians in total. Between fights, you build a salvaged ship.

That is the full extent of the verified feature set. Notably absent from the official page: ship parts, weapon stats, guardian names, sector layouts, unlock costs, and any kind of code system. If a search result promises you a Void Runners tier list or a redeem code, that content is not grounded in anything the developer has published.

## Why the beam changes how you play

Most twin-stick co-op games treat other players as independent damage sources who happen to share a screen. Void Runners inverts that. Because the beam only cuts where it sweeps, your damage output is a function of geometry between two ships, not of a single player's aim. Three practical consequences follow from the store description alone:

- Flying apart extends the beam's reach but thins your coverage and makes revives harder.
- Flying together concentrates damage but forfeits the "swinging the web" crossfire the page describes.
- Dodging is a shared problem. If one pilot breaks formation to avoid fire, the beam moves with them, and the other pilot's safe spot may stop being safe.

None of this is speculation about specific stats. It is the logical read of "where you fly relative to your crew is the core decision." Players who have watched the trailer or followed community discussion tend to describe the same tension, but those are third-party observations, not developer documentation, and they should be treated as impressions rather than confirmed mechanics.

## Revives, escape pods, and the cost of spreading out

Reviving crew from escape pods is the one recovery mechanic named in the official description. What it implies is a risk-reward loop: dying does not necessarily remove a player from the run, but it does remove their ship from the beam web, which weakens everyone still flying. A crew that spreads out for coverage is also a crew that may struggle to reach a downed pilot quickly.

The store page does not state how long a pod persists, whether revives are limited, or whether a solo pilot gets any equivalent safety net. Those details are simply not public. When the game ships, the fastest way to verify them is to deliberately lose a ship in the first sector and observe the timer, then check whether the behavior changes in later sectors.
