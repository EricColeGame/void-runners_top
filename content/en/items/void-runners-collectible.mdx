## Why Positioning Beats Firepower

The beam mechanic changes the usual co-op shooter math. In most twin-stick games, each player is an independent damage source and cooperation is optional efficiency. Here, the beam is a shared object. It exists between two ships, which means its coverage depends on both pilots at once.

That produces a few practical consequences, all of which follow directly from the advertised "spread out and swing the web" phrasing:

- Distance matters. Two ships far apart create a long sweep; two ships close together create a short, dense zone.
- Rotation matters. Turning changes what the beam covers, so movement doubles as aiming.
- Crossfire is a formation decision. The description names crossfire explicitly, which suggests overlapping beam paths are a designed payoff rather than an accident.
- Revives cost position. Escape pods mean a downed player is a location on the map, not just a timer. Someone has to fly there while the beam web weakens.

None of this is published as a stat table, and it would be misleading to pretend otherwise. There are no official numbers for beam length, damage, or revive time. What the store page gives you is the design intent: where you fly relative to your crew is the core decision.

## Co-op on a Couch vs. Online

The listing says one to four pilots on one couch or online, which is a meaningful distinction from games that only support networked play. Local play with beams is a different experience from online play for a simple reason: you can see your partner's screen, or at least their ship, without a camera callout. In online sessions, you will likely need to talk about position constantly, because the beam is invisible to anyone not looking at both ships.

This is reasoning from the advertised features, not from hands-on play. The game is not released, so there is no verified report of how netcode handles beam synchronization or whether local and online modes differ in any mechanical way. Those details are not documented.
