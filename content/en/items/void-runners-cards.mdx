If you searched for "Void Runners" and landed on pages about a synthwave platformer, a spaceship crew sim, or a dungeon crawler starring a girl named Gray, you are in the wrong place. Void Runners is a distinct, currently unreleased Steam game from developer and publisher Aaron Reisman. It is a twin-stick roguelike built for one to four players on a couch or online, and its defining idea is simple: your ship is tethered to a crewmate's ship by a beam, and that beam destroys whatever it sweeps. Where you fly is not just about dodging. It is about where the laser line lands.

## The Core Mechanic: Ships, Beams, and Positioning

The official store description states that each pair of ships is joined by a beam that shreds anything it sweeps. That single sentence drives most of the game's decision-making. Two players who fly side by side cover a narrow strip. Two players who swing wide create a moving web of laser that covers far more ground but leaves gaps, and gaps are where enemies and projectiles get through.

Read that as a cooperative geometry problem rather than a shooting problem. Aim matters, but relative position matters more, because the beam exists in the space between two moving objects. The store page also mentions spreading out and swinging the web, plus crossfire, which confirms that the intended play pattern is deliberate spacing rather than clumping into a tight ball of ships.

The page lists dodging and reviving crew from escape pods as features, which tells you two things. First, death is expected — this is a roguelike, and runs end. Second, a downed pilot is not a permanent loss, so a squad can recover from a bad swing instead of restarting instantly. How revives actually work in practice is not documented, and you should treat any specific claim about revive timers or pod mechanics as unverified until the game is playable.

## Run Structure: Three Sectors, Four Jumps, Three Guardians

The verified description is unusually specific here, and it is worth quoting the shape of it precisely: a run crosses three sectors of four jumps, ending at a guardian. Three sectors, three guardians deep.

That gives you a useful mental model before launch. A full run is not an endless survival mode with a score counter. It is a bounded journey with three checkpoints, and the guardian at the end of each sector is the wall you have to break through. Four jumps per sector means the pacing between guardians is short — likely short enough that a squad can lose a run quickly and start another without much downtime, which suits a couch co-op game where someone is passing a controller.

What lives inside those jumps — enemy types, hazards, environmental gimmicks — is not published. The store page does not name a single sector, guardian, or encounter. If you are planning to play with three friends, the practical takeaway from the advertised features is to talk constantly about spacing, not just about targets.
