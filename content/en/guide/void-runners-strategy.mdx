## The one mechanic that explains everything else

Most twin-stick roguelikes ask where you point. Void Runners asks where you are relative to your crew, because your damage output is a function of geometry, not aim. Two linked ships sweeping a beam across a room cover ground that no single ship can cover, and the same beam that kills enemies also defines the space your teammate must avoid or exploit.

The official description on the [Void Runners Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5334520/) frames this as spreading out and swinging the web, with crossfire, dodging, and reviving crew from escape pods as part of the loop. That is a cooperative spatial puzzle dressed as a shooter. If you have played twin-stick games solo for years, the adjustment is not mechanical skill. It is learning to think of your teammate's position as a resource you are continuously spending.

## Couch co-op versus online, and why the difference matters

The description lists one to four pilots on one couch or online. Those are not equivalent experiences for a game built on beam geometry.

On a shared screen, you can see your partner's ship and your own at the same time, and that changes how you plan. The beam is visible to both of you simultaneously, so coordinating a sweep is a matter of talking, not describing. Online play adds a layer of translation: you have to say what you are doing rather than simply doing it in view of someone. That is a real cost in a game where relative position is the whole decision.

If you are deciding how to gather a group, the honest answer is that couch play is structurally easier to coordinate, but the store page does not rank the modes or suggest one is favored. Choose based on who is actually available, and verify matchmaking or lobby details on the official page closer to launch, since the description does not specify them.
