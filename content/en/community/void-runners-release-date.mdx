Void Runners is an upcoming co-op twin-stick roguelike from developer and publisher Aaron Reisman, listed on Steam (app 5334520) as coming soon with a Q1 2027 release window. The hook is unusual enough that it is worth explaining carefully: your ship is tethered to another player's ship by a beam, and that beam destroys whatever it sweeps. That single rule reframes almost every decision in the game, from where you fly to how far you drift from your crew. This article sticks to what the official store page actually says, flags what is community observation, and is blunt about what nobody outside the studio can confirm yet.

## The Beam Is the Core Mechanic, Not a Weapon Slot

Most twin-stick shooters treat your guns as the source of damage. In Void Runners, the advertised system is a beam that links pairs of ships and shreds anything it sweeps. Read that literally and the implication is large: damage is a function of geometry, not of a stat sheet. Two pilots flying apart create a long line of destruction between them. Two pilots flying close create a short one.

That means the interesting question in a fight is rarely "what am I shooting at." It is "where is the other end of my beam, and what is between us." The [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5334520/) describes the intended play as spreading out and swinging the web, using crossfire, dodging incoming fire, and reviving crew from escape pods. Every one of those verbs assumes the tether exists.

## Why Formation Beats Aim in This Design

If the beam is the damage source, then positioning is the skill. A few practical consequences follow from the advertised description alone, without needing any unpublished numbers.

Spreading out widens the swept area but also thins your local coverage and slows your ability to react to a threat on one flank. Clustering tightens the beam into a near-point-blank cutter but removes the "web" element that the store description explicitly calls out. The space between those extremes is the actual game.

For a squad of four, ships pair off. That means a four-player run is not one big formation; it is two linked pairs that presumably need to coordinate their own webs. The store description does not detail how pairing is assigned, whether it can change mid-run, or what happens when one member of a pair is destroyed. Those are reasonable questions to bring to the game itself rather than assume an answer.

| Situation | Likely trade-off (from advertised features) |
| --- | --- |
| Ships spread wide | Long beam sweeps more area, but each pilot is more exposed |
| Ships flying tight | Compact beam, easier mutual cover, narrower total sweep |
| Two pairs of two | More total beams, but coordination cost across the squad |
| Reviving from an escape pod | Requires a pilot to disengage from the web to recover a downed crewmate |

The bottom row is the one people underestimate. The description says crew are revived from escape pods, which means a death does not simply remove a player from the equation; it creates a recovery objective that pulls someone away from the formation. How that interacts with an active beam pair is not documented.

## Three Sectors, Four Jumps, One Guardian

The run structure is one of the few things the store page states concretely: a run crosses three sectors, each of which appears to contain four jumps, ending at a guardian. Three sectors and three guardians deep, per the official description.

What that gives you is a predictable rhythm. You know roughly how many engagements sit between you and the end of a sector, and you know a guardian is waiting. What the page does not give you is guardian names, attack patterns, sector themes, or any sense of difficulty curve. Anyone publishing "guardian strategies" for Void Runners right now is working from assumption, not documentation.

The same applies to the ship. The description mentions building a salvaged ship, which strongly suggests a progression layer between and during runs. It does not publish ship parts, upgrade trees, unlock costs, or stat values. If you see a guide listing specific modules or drop rates, treat it as speculation.
