Most co-op games ask you to shoot the same enemy at the same time. Void Runners asks you to think about where you are standing relative to everyone else. That single shift is the whole pitch, and it is worth understanding before the game arrives, because it changes how you plan a run, how you position in a fight, and how you talk to your crew.

This article sticks to what developer Aaron Reisman has published on the game's [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5334520/), and clearly flags anything that is speculation, third-party observation, or simply not documented yet. Void Runners was announced for Q1 2027 and is marked "coming soon." It has not been released.

One clarification up front, because search results are messy on this name: this is not Solar Ash, not Void Crew, not Void Stranger, and not Starfinder. It shares a word with several of them and nothing else.

## What the developer has confirmed

The Steam listing describes Void Runners as a co-op salvage raid for one to four pilots, playable on a single couch or online. The defining mechanic is a beam that links each pair of ships. The beam shreds anything it sweeps. That means the beam is not just a visual connector or a buff aura. It is damage, and it applies along the line between two players.

From that one rule, several advertised behaviors follow naturally:

- Spreading out and swinging the web. Players are meant to move apart and rotate, dragging the tether across enemies rather than aiming directly at them.
- Crossfire. Overlapping beams create zones that enemies cross at their peril.
- Dodging. The same movement that aims your beam also exposes or protects your ship.
- Reviving crew from escape pods. Downed pilots are not permanently out; there is a recovery step.
- Building a salvaged ship. Progression is framed around assembling a vessel from what you recover.

Structurally, a run crosses three sectors of four jumps each, ending at a guardian. The store page mentions three guardians in total. That is the entire documented shape of a run.

## Why the tether changes your decisions

In a normal twin-stick roguelike, your position matters because of what you can shoot and what can shoot you. In Void Runners, your position also determines where your damage currently is, and that damage belongs to two people at once.

Consider the practical consequences. If you and your partner fly parallel, your beam sweeps a lane. If you orbit a target from opposite sides, you cut across it twice per rotation. If you cross paths, the beam momentarily collapses to nothing, which is the opposite of what you want during a damage window. Every one of those is a spatial decision, not a trigger-pull decision.

With four pilots, the geometry gets harder to hold in your head. The store description talks about a "web" of beams, which implies multiple links interacting. The developer has not published the exact pairing rules at four players, so whether beams form a fixed chain, a full mesh, or something reassigned mid-run is **not documented**. That is a detail to watch for in patch notes, an official trailer, or the in-game tutorial.

The same caution applies to ship parts, weapon stats, and guardian names. The store page does not publish them. Anyone quoting specific numbers right now is guessing, and you should treat third-party guides on an unreleased game as secondhand at best.

## Playing the beam instead of the trigger

If you want to prepare for Void Runners before launch, the useful mental exercise is not memorizing builds. It is practicing awareness of your ally's screen position. Twin-stick veterans tend to fixate on the reticle; this game rewards pilots who keep a second mental marker on their partner.

A few transferable habits from other couch co-op games:

- Call your movement, not just your targets. "Rotating left" tells your partner where the beam is going.
- Treat the beam as a resource with a location. Ask where it is before you ask where the enemies are.
- Give downed pilots a lane. Escape pod revival suggests recovery happens in space, so a crewmate flying a tight pattern may be the one who can reach it.

None of this is a guarantee of success. The game is unreleased, balance is unknown, and the developer has not described difficulty scaling for solo play beyond confirming one player is supported.

## Solo, couch, and online in the same design

Supporting one to four players on one couch *and* online is a real design constraint, and the store page states both without elaboration. For a beam-based game, the interesting unknown is how a single pilot experiences the core mechanic when there is no second ship to link to. The description does not explain it.

If you are planning a group, the practical advice is to decide early whether you are a couch crew or a remote crew, because voice communication carries most of the load in a game where relative position is the primary input. Online play usually needs clearer callouts than shared-screen play, where you can simply glance sideways.
