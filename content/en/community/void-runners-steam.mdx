## What Void Runners Is, in One Paragraph

Void Runners is a twin-stick roguelike for one to four players, played either on a single couch or online. Each pair of ships is joined by a beam, and that beam shreds whatever it sweeps. Because your weapon is partly a geometry problem, where you fly relative to your crewmates is the central decision, not an afterthought. A run crosses three sectors of four jumps each and ends at a guardian. That is the complete advertised loop as of the October 2026 Steam listing.

Note what this is not. Void Runners is not Solar Ash, not Void Crew, not Void Stranger, and not Starfinder. The shared word "void" pulls those games into the same search results, but they share nothing else with this project.

## The Beam Is the Whole Argument

Most twin-stick co-op games hand each player a gun and let them point it. Void Runners replaces the gun with a relationship. If two ships are linked, the line between them cuts. Sweep that line across enemies and they die. The design consequence is that your movement is your attack, and your teammate's movement is half of your attack.

The description names a few things that follow from this:

- **Spreading out and swinging the web.** More distance between linked ships covers more area, but a beam stretched too far presumably covers it less cleanly or less often.
- **Crossfire.** Overlapping beams from different pairs create zones where movement is punishing for enemies.
- **Dodging.** A beam does nothing for you if you are dead, so positioning has a defensive cost as well as an offensive one.
- **Reviving crew from escape pods.** Downed players are not permanently out; they are recovered, which matters a lot in a four-player run where one bad sweep wipes the web.

The description also mentions building a salvaged ship. That is as far as the store text goes. Which parts exist, what they do, whether they are picked up mid-run or between runs, and how salvaging is scored are all undocumented. If you want to know before release, wishlist the game on Steam and read patch notes after it ships; the developer has not published a parts list, and no third-party guide can have one either, because the game is not out.

## Playing with Four People Changes the Math

With two pilots there is one beam. With four there are two pairs, which means two beams, and those beams can be arranged independently. That is the difference between a line and a shape.

The store description frames the core decision as where you fly relative to your crew, and four-player play is where that decision gets expensive. A pair that hugs tight is safe and cuts almost nothing. A pair that stretches wide sweeps a corridor but leaves both ships exposed and makes the beam's path through the arena harder to predict for the other pair. Nobody has published an optimal formation, and honestly, a formation that works in one sector may not work in the next, because enemies and arena geometry are not documented either.

If you are planning to play on a couch, the practical advice is simply to agree on who is anchored and who is swinging before the run starts. Agreeing mid-run, while a guardian is on screen, tends to go badly.
