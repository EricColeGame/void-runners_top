## What Void Runners Is, Per the Store Page

The store description frames Void Runners as co-op salvage raids for one to four pilots, played on one couch or online. Two ideas carry the whole pitch:

- **Beams link ships.** Each pair of ships is joined by a beam, and that beam shreds anything it sweeps.
- **Position is the decision.** Because the beam is a line between two moving players, where you fly relative to your crew is the core tactical choice. Spreading out stretches the beam; swinging the web changes what it cuts.

The page also mentions crossfire, dodging, reviving crew from escape pods, and building a salvaged ship. Structurally, a run crosses three sectors of four jumps and ends at a guardian, with three guardians in total.

That is the entire advertised feature set. It is enough to understand the loop, and not enough to build a spreadsheet.

## Why the Beam Changes How You Play

Most twin-stick co-op games treat players as independent guns that happen to share a screen. Void Runners inverts that. Your damage is a relationship, not a weapon.

Think through the consequences. If you and a partner fly close together, the beam is short — safe, easy to aim, but covering very little ground. Spread apart and the beam becomes a long sweeping line that can cut across a wide arc, which is exactly what you want against spread-out salvage or a crowd. The cost is that a long beam is harder to steer precisely and drags both players' positioning into each other's problems.

Add a third or fourth pilot and it becomes a web rather than a line. Now the interesting question is not "where is the enemy" but "which of us should move to change the shape of the web." A player who drifts off to chase a target is not just risking themselves; they are redrawing everyone's coverage.

Two advertised details reinforce this. Crossfire suggests the design rewards overlapping angles rather than a single stacked gun line. Escape-pod revives suggest that dying is expected and recoverable, which softens the usual roguelike penalty and keeps a four-player run from collapsing when one pilot makes a bad read. Both are store-page statements, not tested mechanics — the game is unreleased.

## Three Sectors, Four Jumps, One Guardian

The advertised run structure is tidy and worth repeating precisely: three sectors, four jumps in each, ending at a guardian, three guardians deep. That means twelve jumps between you and the end of a run, assuming the store page is describing a straight path.

The practical read is that Void Runners is aiming for a shorter, denser roguelike run than the genre's long-form entries — the kind of structure that suits a couch session where people rotate in and out. Twelve encounters plus three bosses is a shape you can finish in one sitting, though the page does not state a target run time, and you should not assume one.

What the page does not tell you is arguably more important. There is no published information on:

- What happens between jumps (shop, choice screen, rest, or nothing)
- Whether guardians scale with player count
- Whether sectors have themes, factions, or biome differences
- Whether ships are chosen before a run or assembled during it, beyond the phrase "building a salvaged ship"

"Building a salvaged ship" is the one phrase that hints at meta-progression — collecting wreckage across runs and assembling something — but the store page never says whether that ship persists between runs or is assembled within a single one. Do not let a third-party guide tell you otherwise; there is no developer documentation to support either reading.

Void Runners supports one to four pilots, which means solo play is in scope.
