## What Are Void Runners? A Quick Primer

Void Runners drops you into a fractured stretch of space where the floor is temporary and the clock never stops. Every run asks the same question: how long can you keep moving before the void swallows the path behind you? It borrows ideas from speedrun-friendly platformers and the wider [endless runner genre cataloged on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/tags/en/Runner/), then layers on a collapse mechanic that punishes hesitation harder than it punishes mistakes.

That is exactly why this void runners beginner guide focuses on movement, timing, and upgrade choices instead of lore. Runs are short by design, so most new players measure progress in seconds rather than levels — which means small improvements compound quickly.

One note on accuracy before we go further: exact ability names, costs, and numbers shift between modes and updates. Treat everything in this void runners beginner guide as relative guidance, and confirm the specifics in the tooltips of the build you are playing.

## Core Mechanics That Decide Your Runs

Understanding three systems will carry you further than any single trick.

### Momentum Is Your Real Health Bar

Speed builds while you hold forward and while you land cleanly off jumps. Sharp turns, hard stops, and clipping walls drain it. In practice, this means you should plan your turns one obstacle ahead, not at the moment you reach them. Players who steer early keep their speed; players who react late lose it and get caught by the collapse.

### The Collapse Timer

The void eats the path behind you at a steady rate, and that rate climbs the longer a run lasts. Backtracking is almost always fatal, and so is doubling back for a shard you already passed. If you have to choose between a risky pickup and staying ahead of the collapse, staying ahead wins nearly every time.

### Shards, Banking, and Risk

Shards spawn on dangerous lines — near ledges, over gaps, and along walls that require a wall-run. They typically only count if you bank the run, which creates a risk-versus-reward decision on every pickup. Community reports suggest that new players who chase every shard die far more often than those who take the safe line and grab only the easy ones. Start conservative, then add risk as your movement tightens up.

### Hazard Types at a Glance

| Hazard type | What it looks like | How to handle it |
|---|---|---|
| Static barriers | Fixed blocks and pillars in the path | Read them two obstacles early and drift around them |
| Sweeping hazards | Moving beams or rotating arms | Time a dash through the gap, never a jump |
| Collapsing floor | Tiles that drop after one step | Keep forward pressure; never pause to look |
| Void zones | Dark instant-fail patches at edges | Give them a wide berth instead of hugging the line |
| Pursuit wave | The collapse itself | Never stop, never reverse |

## Movement and Controls Compared

Every traversal option has a best-case moment. Using the right one at the wrong time is the most common source of early deaths.

| Move | What it does | Best moment to use | Beginner mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dash | Short burst of forward speed | Crossing gaps and dodging sweeping hazards | Spamming it and arriving with no charge |
| Slide | Low profile, maintains momentum | Under low barriers and into tight turns | Sliding uphill or into a wall |
| Double jump | Extends air time | Chaining platforms with uneven spacing | Using the second jump too early |
| Wall-run | Turns vertical surfaces into paths | Long corridors with side walls | Entering too low and sliding off |
| Grapple | Pulls you toward a fixed point | Crossing wide void gaps | Firing at moving targets |

If you are brand new, master dash and slide before anything else. Those two moves cover the majority of early obstacles, and both reward timing over reflexes. Wall-runs and grapples are higher-value once you can read a level's shape at speed, but they punish sloppy input much harder.

## Upgrade Priorities for New Players

Upgrade menus are where beginners quietly lose runs. Spending early resources on score multipliers feels productive but does nothing to keep you alive.

| Upgrade category | Priority | Why it matters early |
|---|---|---|
| Movement and stamina | Highest | Longer dashes and more air control directly extend runs |
| Shard collection | High | Magnet range and pickup radius reduce risky detours |
| Shields or extra lives | Medium-high | One free mistake per run is enormous while learning |
| Score multipliers | Medium | Only worth it once your average run is stable |
| Cosmetics | Lowest | No gameplay effect; save these for later |

A simple rule: if an upgrade does not help you stay on the path, it can wait. Revisit your build every few sessions, because the upgrades that matter change once your movement stops being the bottleneck.

## Common Mistakes and a First-Week Practice Plan

Most beginner problems repeat across players. Here are the big ones and the fix for each.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Looking at your character | You see obstacles too late | Focus your eyes on the middle of the screen, ahead of the player |
| Hoarding dashes | The collapse catches you | Dash early and often; it recharges faster than you think |
| Fighting the camera | Speed drops and turns feel sluggish | Let the camera settle; steer with small inputs |
| Restarting instantly | No learning happens between runs | Spend five seconds naming what killed you |
| Copying expert routes | Those lines assume max speed | Build your own safe line first, then optimize |

Practice is more effective when it has a target. This plan fits into roughly 15 to 20 minutes a day.

| Session | Focus | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Dash and slide timing only | Clear the first hazard set without stopping |
| Day 2 | Reading ahead | Survive 60 seconds without looking at your character |
| Day 3 | Safe shard routes | Bank a run with at least half the available shards |
| Day 4 | Wall-runs | Chain two wall-runs in a single run |
| Day 5 | Speed over safety | Beat your day 3 distance while ignoring shards |
| Day 6–7 | Combine | Run a full route with shards and no deaths |

Consistency beats marathon sessions here. Three short, focused runs teach more than one long session where you are tired and reacting instead of reading. Many players also find that recording a run and watching it back reveals hesitation they never noticed while playing.

## FAQ

### How long before I stop dying in the first minute?

Most players need a handful of focused sessions, not dozens of runs. The turning point usually comes when you stop looking at your character and start reading the path ahead. Once that habit sticks, run length tends to jump quickly.

### What should I upgrade first?

Movement and stamina, then shard collection, then shields. Score multipliers and cosmetics are the last things to touch. If an upgrade does not keep you on the path, it is not a beginner upgrade.

### Do I need to collect every shard?

No. Shards only matter if you bank the run, and the risky ones cost more than they pay while you are still learning. Take the easy pickups, ignore the ledge-hugging ones, and revisit them once your movement is reliable.

### If I take one thing from this void runners beginner guide, what should it be?

Keep moving forward. Nearly every system in the game rewards forward pressure — momentum, the collapse timer, and shard placement all push you in the same direction. Hesitation is the real enemy, and the players who survive longest are the ones who commit to a line and adjust early rather than late.
