## What the Steam Page Confirms

The core concept is a beam that links two ships. Each pair of ships is joined by a beam, and that beam shreds whatever it sweeps. This is the whole design in one sentence, and it changes how you think about movement. You are not just dodging and shooting. You are dragging a cutting line across the screen, and the line's position is decided by two players at once.

The page describes the flow as co-op salvage raids for one to four pilots, playable on one couch or online. The language covers spreading out and swinging the web, crossfire, dodging, and reviving crew from escape pods. Runs are described as crossing three sectors of four jumps each, ending at a guardian. The phrase "three sectors and three guardians deep" appears in the description, which lines up with three sectors, three guardians.

That is the verified set. Notice what is absent: no ship parts, no weapon stats, no guardian names, no sector layouts, no unlock costs, and no code system. If you have seen a guide or a video claiming to list any of those, it is either speculation or it is describing a different game.

## Why the Beam Is the Real Decision

Most twin-stick co-op games ask where you point. Void Runners appears to ask where you are relative to your crew, because the beam is a physical object with two endpoints, and it cuts. The store description's own phrasing, spreading out and swinging the web with four pilots, implies that distance between ships is a resource you spend deliberately rather than a mistake you avoid.

The practical reasoning falls out of that: if you and a partner drift apart, the beam gets longer and sweeps a wider arc. If you stack up, the beam collapses to almost nothing and stops doing work. Crossfire matters because beams plus your own shots create overlapping danger zones. Reviving from escape pods matters because a dead pilot is not just a lost gun, it is a missing endpoint, which means the surviving player's beam geometry changes when someone goes down.

None of this is a strategy guide, because the developer has not published numbers. Treat it as a way to read the description rather than a promise that any particular formation wins runs.
