## What Void Runners Actually Is

According to the official Steam description, Void Runners is a co-op salvage raid game for one to four pilots, playable on a single couch or online. Each pair of ships is joined by a beam, and that beam shreds anything it sweeps. That single mechanic drives everything else: spreading out and swinging the web of beams, catching enemies in crossfire, dodging return fire, and reviving crew from escape pods.

Structurally, a run crosses three sectors and four jumps per sector, ending at a guardian. There are three guardians in total, one per sector. You also build a salvaged ship over the course of a run, which implies progression inside a single attempt rather than only between runs, though the store page does not spell out how that build system works.

That is the full scope of what the developer has published. Notably absent: ship parts, weapon stats, boss names, upgrade costs, and any kind of code system. If you see a guide listing "best beam loadouts" or a cheat code list, treat it as unverified at best.

## Why the Beam Is the Whole Game

Twin-stick shooters usually ask one question: where do I aim? Void Runners asks a harder one: where do I fly, and where does that put my beam relative to my crew?

Because beams link pairs of ships, your position is simultaneously your offense and your teammate's offense. Two players who drift apart stretch the beam across a wide arc, which sweeps more area but leaves gaps the beam cannot reach. Two players who stay tight concentrate damage but cover less ground and give enemies a predictable line to shoot down.

This is why "where you fly relative to your crew is the core decision" is not marketing fluff, it is a mechanical description. In a four-player group, you have two beams, so you have to decide which pairs link up and how those two beams interact. The store page mentions crossfire explicitly, which suggests overlapping beams do something meaningful, though the exact behavior is not documented.

## Solo Play and the One-Pilot Problem

The game supports one to four pilots. With one pilot, there is no pair, so the beam mechanic as described cannot function the same way. The store page does not explain how solo play handles this. It is possible the solo ship carries its own beam, or that solo runs use a different setup entirely. That detail is simply not public yet, and anyone claiming to know the solo rules is guessing.

If you plan to buy this primarily for solo play, the honest answer is that the official page does not yet justify that purchase. If you plan to play with two to four people on a couch, the described design is aimed directly at you.
