## What Void Runners Actually Is

The Steam store description frames Void Runners as a co-op salvage raid for one to four pilots, playable on one couch or online. The defining mechanic is a beam that links each pair of ships. That beam shreds anything it sweeps, which means your position relative to your crewmates is not cosmetic. It is the core decision the game asks you to make moment to moment.

The structure is a run across three sectors of four jumps, ending at a guardian. There are three guardians in total across a full run. Along the way the description mentions spreading out and swinging the web, crossfire, dodging, reviving crew from escape pods, and building a salvaged ship. That is the complete published feature set.

Worth stating plainly, because search results are messy: this is not Solar Ash, not Void Crew, not Void Stranger, and not Starfinder. Those are separate games that share a word or a genre tag. Void Runners has its own Steam app ID, 5334520, and its own developer.

## The Beam Is the Whole Game

Most twin-stick co-op games treat positioning as a damage-avoidance problem. Void Runners appears to treat it as a damage-dealing problem too. If a beam connects two ships and destroys what it sweeps, then the shape your team forms is a weapon. Two players flying parallel create a moving line of destruction. Four players can in principle form a wider web and sweep a larger area, but only if they coordinate, and only if they accept the risk of crossing paths.

The store description supports three ideas without giving numbers:

- Spreading out and swinging the web, which implies the beam geometry changes as pilots move.
- Crossfire, which implies overlapping beam angles matter.
- Dodging, which implies enemy fire punishes sloppy formation.

What the page does not publish is any of the arithmetic. There is no beam length, no damage value, no tick rate, no cooldown. Those are not documented, and no third-party guide can legitimately supply them for an unreleased build.
