"Void Runners" is easy to confuse with other games. Searches for the term turn up a void-themed platformer, a co-op spaceship sim, a puzzle game, and a tabletop RPG system, none of which are the same project. The game this article covers is the one on Steam as app 5334520: a twin-stick roguelike from developer and publisher Aaron Reisman, announced for Q1 2027 and still listed as coming soon. Because it has not shipped, most of what people want to know — parts, stats, boss names, codes — does not exist yet in any official form. ## What the Steam page actually promises

The official store description is the only authoritative source right now, and it is short. It describes co-op salvage raids for one to four pilots, playable on one couch or online. Each pair of ships is joined by a beam that shreds anything it sweeps, which makes positioning relative to your crew the central decision rather than a secondary one. The page also mentions spreading out and swinging the web, crossfire, dodging, reviving crew from escape pods, and building a salvaged ship.

Structurally, a run crosses three sectors of four jumps each and ends at a guardian, of which there are three. That is the full published scope. The page does not list ship parts, weapon stats, guardian names, sector layouts, unlock costs, or any code system. If a guide or video claims otherwise, treat it as speculation until the game is out or the developer posts something official.

The beam mechanic is worth dwelling on, because it is the one thing that genuinely distinguishes this game from other twin-stick roguelikes. In most games of this genre, your position matters because of what you can shoot. Here, your position matters because of what your beam crosses. Two ships linked by a beam form a moving line segment, and that segment damages what it touches. Flying in parallel sweeps a wide lane; flying apart stretches the beam thin and may leave gaps; crossing paths creates a scissors effect. None of the specifics — beam width, damage values, whether the beam breaks at a maximum distance — are published. You can confirm them by watching the store page trailer and by testing them yourself if you get access at launch.

## One to four players: what co-op changes

The advertised player count is one to four, and the store page treats couch and online play as equivalent options. With more players, the beam network gets more complex. Two ships make one beam. Three or four ships can make several, depending on how the game pairs them, and the description's phrase "swinging the web" suggests that the arrangement of ships matters more than any individual ship's firepower.

## Reviving crew and escape pods

Reviving crew from escape pods is the one failure-and-recovery mechanic the page names outright. It implies that a downed pilot is not simply out of the run; there is a pod, and someone can reach it. What the page does not say is how long a pod persists, whether reviving costs anything, or whether a solo pilot has any equivalent. Community discussion has not settled these points because there is no build to test.

A practical note for anyone planning to play with a group: this mechanic makes the beam web a rescue tool as well as a weapon. If a pod sits behind enemy fire, the safest route to it may be a beam sweep rather than a direct approach. That is inference from the advertised description, not a documented tactic, and it should be verified in play.
